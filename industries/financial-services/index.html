Industry

AI for financial services

AI agent governance and architecture for banks, credit unions, insurers, fintechs and payment companies.

Overview

Financial firms have to be able to explain AI-driven decisions and back the explanation with evidence, because those decisions affect customers and regulators review them. Agents add another requirement. Once software can initiate a payment or change a record, each of those actions has to be traceable to a named person at the time it happened.

Our work is aimed at mid-sized banks, credit unions, insurers, fintechs and payment companies whose use of agents has grown faster than their controls. It usually begins with a fixed-scope governance assessment mapped to SR 11-7, SOX, DORA and the EU AI Act. Later stages cover approval and audit architecture, compliance controls for AI-assisted engineering, and knowledge graphs for agents to draw on.

Firms subject to DORA or the EU AI Act tend to start sooner, since both expect controls to be demonstrated with evidence.

Questions

Common questions

Which regulations apply to AI agents in financial services?

Usually several. In the US that includes model risk guidance such as SR 11-7 and SOX controls over financial reporting. PCI DSS applies wherever card data is involved, DORA sets ICT risk rules for EU financial entities, and the EU AI Act covers AI systems used in the EU. All of them assume you can show what a system did and who was accountable for it.

How can banks use AI agents safely?

By keeping execution and authority separate. Agents prepare and carry out work, but each consequential action, such as a payment, needs a signed approval from a named person. The approval covers the exact parameters, expires quickly and can be revoked; it is checked before execution and recorded.
